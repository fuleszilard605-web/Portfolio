01 / About me

Technology should work for people.

My background combines two disciplines that I believe are most valuable when considered together: understanding human behaviour and building the technology people use.

As a third-year Psychology and Computing student at University College Cork, I'm particularly interested in how research, design, and software development can come together to create more intuitive, engaging, and useful digital experiences.

Studying psychology has encouraged me to look beyond whether a piece of software simply works. I am interested in how people perceive information, make decisions, interact with their environment, and respond to the systems around them. I want to use that perspective to help identify what users actually need, rather than assuming that more features automatically lead to a better experience.

My computing studies allow me to approach these questions from a practical, technical perspective. Through academic projects, I have developed experience with Python, C#, HTML, and JavaScript, as well as object-oriented programming, debugging, and building functional software. I have also explored interface design using Figma and developed a low-fidelity functional prototype using Unity and C#.

I am particularly drawn to experiences where software and the physical world intersect. Devices with dedicated displays offer interesting opportunities to provide useful information and controls without unnecessarily interrupting what someone is doing. I am interested in how the timing, presentation, and accessibility of information can influence whether these interactions feel genuinely helpful or become a distraction.

This is what attracts me to user experience work involving gaming, applications, and connected devices. I enjoy the challenge of considering both the person using a product and the technical constraints involved in delivering it. I want to develop my ability to translate research findings into design decisions, communicate ideas through interactive wireframes, and build prototypes that allow interaction concepts to be explored and tested.

I bring an interdisciplinary perspective, a willingness to learn unfamiliar technologies, and experience working collaboratively on technical projects. I am keen to keep developing my skills in user research, usability-focused design, and prototyping while contributing to products that make technology feel more natural, accessible, and enjoyable.

My guiding principle: understand the user, question assumptions, and use research and prototyping to explore solutions before committing to them.

02 / Areas of interest

Where psychology meets technology.

These are the questions and challenges that motivate me to explore user experience, interaction design, and the development of useful software.

01 — PEOPLE

Understanding user needs

Understanding what users are trying to accomplish, what information matters to them, and what makes an interaction feel intuitive. My psychology background motivates me to consider the reasoning and behaviour behind design decisions.

02 — INTERACTIONS

Seamless user experiences

Exploring how interfaces can provide useful information and accessible controls without disrupting a user's primary activity. I am especially interested in gaming environments, secondary displays, and context-aware interactions.

03 — PROTOTYPES

Turning ideas into something testable

Using wireframes and functional prototypes to make ideas tangible. I want to explore how early implementation, feedback, and iteration can help identify interaction problems and improve a design.

04 — INFORMATION

Useful information at the right time

Considering how information is organised and presented, and how people can quickly understand what matters. I am interested in balancing information density, clarity, and accessibility.

05 — ACCESSIBILITY

Designing for different people

Considering how different cognitive abilities, levels of experience, and interaction needs influence the way people use technology. My serious game project introduced me to accessibility-focused development.

06 — EVIDENCE

Research-informed decisions

Connecting psychological research and practical design questions. I am interested in using evidence to explain why an interface may work well, where friction may occur, and what could be explored through further testing.

04 / My approach

From understanding to implementation.

The following is the approach I want to develop further as I combine psychological research, interaction design, and software development.

STEP 01

Understand

Define the problem, identify the people involved, and investigate their goals, behaviours, and potential difficulties.

STEP 02

Explore

Consider alternative approaches, examine interaction patterns, and use wireframes to communicate possible solutions.

STEP 03

Prototype

Build an interactive representation of an idea to investigate its core functionality and explore how its interactions work.

STEP 04

Evaluate & refine

Examine how well a proposed solution addresses the original problem, identify opportunities for improvement, and iterate where appropriate.

This section describes my developing design philosophy, rather than claiming that every project listed above followed this complete process.
